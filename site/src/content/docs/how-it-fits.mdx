millpond sits between the tools that notice events and the tools that do
the work. Each tool stands alone, with its own Claude Code and herdr
plugins, and millpond is the inbox that joins them.

<Family highlight="millpond" />

## The family

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="headwater · releases">
    Reports releases and advisories for what a repository declares. Its
    adapter part, `headwater capture`, prints one capture per new
    release. The release's notes are its excerpt, and its signal asks for
    a bump.
  </Card>
  <Card title="eddy · meetings">
    Lists recorded meetings across meeting services. A part per service
    prints one capture per meeting, with the transcript as raw material
    and no excerpt, so a reader turns it into signals.
  </Card>
  <Card title="sounding · one question">
    Works one question in one session, in a worktree of its own, and ends
    on a conclusion you accept, send back for another round, or drop.
    millpond sends it an item that asks a question, keeps the accepted
    conclusion as the item's record, and makes an item of each piece of
    work it proposes. A finding beyond the question comes back as a
    capture, keyed `sounding/<name>/<n>`.
  </Card>
  <Card title="sluice · landing">
    Tests a change as merged and lands it. The agent an item starts
    submits through sluice, and millpond records the landing against the
    item.
  </Card>
  <Card title="crew · teams and plans">
    Runs teams, plans and bolts, and calls millpond's command line as it
    calls sluice's. Its curator works an inbox through millpond's tools,
    and its planner turns items into chores and units.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Flywheel · the umbrella">
    Built later on the same crates as millpond. It reads the same
    inboxes with no import, and crew becomes Flywheel.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

An adapter whose source needs no other tool ships inside millpond: the
folder drop, where any file in a watched folder is one capture, and the
import that carries existing signal folders in once. An adapter that
wraps another tool's domain ships with that tool, so headwater's and
eddy's parts stay theirs and millpond runs them like anyone else's.

## Three setups, one inbox

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="On its own">
    **Inbox:** a git repository of your own.
    **Curates:** a Claude session through millpond's tools, and you
    accept. **Items go** to one worktree each, landed by sluice, or a
    question to a sounding.
  </Card>
  <Card title="With crew">
    **Inbox:** the partition's flywheel branch in crew's state
    repository. **Curates:** crew's curator through the same tools, and
    the operator decides each item on crew's rail. **Items go** to crew's
    planner, then a team, landed by sluice.
  </Card>
  <Card title="With Flywheel">
    **Inbox:** the instance's state repository, the same files.
    **Curates:** Flywheel's curation session. **Items go** to Flywheel's
    units and bolts.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

Moving from one setup to the next changes the host and the curator. The
adapters and the inbox stay where they are, so nothing is captured twice
and Flywheel imports nothing when it takes over.

## What millpond never does

- **It never judges what a capture says.** A reader session reads a
  capture into signals, and a curation session proposes moves. A model
  runs in those two places and nowhere else.
- **It never plans across items.** crew's planner orders items across
  teams today, and Flywheel's does later.
- **It never works a question.** A sounding does, and millpond keeps the
  conclusion you accept.
- **It never knows a source.** An adapter lists its source and keys its
  events. millpond takes whatever lines it is handed, from any adapter.
- **It never lets raw material into git.** A transcript stays on the
  host behind a pointer.

## Built once, used everywhere

millpond and Flywheel share their foundation, flywheel-next's crates.
They hold the capture contract's types and key rules, the signal, move
and item records, the built-in adapters, the five-field cron schedule
and the conformance pack, and both hosts take them. One conformance pack
proves every host and every adapter part. Raw banking, the checks on a
capture and the verbatim check on a reader's excerpt are millpond's, and
Flywheel takes them in turn.
