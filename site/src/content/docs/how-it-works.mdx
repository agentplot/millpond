An event reaches millpond as a **capture**. millpond keeps captures in an
**inbox**, a git repository or a branch in one. Each capture becomes
**signals**: one when the capture carries its own excerpt, or as many as
a reader session finds in it. Curation gives every signal one
move and joins signals into **items**. millpond sends an item to one
worktree, where an agent does the work, or an item that asks a question
to a sounding, which ends on a conclusion you accept.

## One way in

An adapter knows one source. It lists the source's events, keys each
one, fetches the raw material and writes it to a directory millpond
hands it. Then it prints one JSON line per event on standard output:

```json
{"capture": {"key": "release/mise/2026.10.10", "source": "release", "event_at": "2026-10-10T14:02:11Z", "raw": ["mise-2026.10.10/notes.md"], "excerpt": {"text": "…the notes, whole…", "kind": "ask"}}}
{"cursor": "2026-10-17T04:30:05Z"}
{"done": {"listed": 14, "failed": [{"what": "github:jdx/mise", "why": "HTTP 502"}]}}
```

The **key** is the event's identity, the same from every host and every
run. The **cursor** is the adapter's own bookmark, which millpond hands
back on the next run. The **done** line ends a complete run with its
counts and its failures. An adapter keeps no list of what it reported,
since millpond dedupes by key.

`millpond run <part>` starts an adapter on a schedule and keeps its
cursor and status. `millpond land` takes the same lines on standard
input from anything that pipes them in: a deployer reporting a failed
step, a script, a person. `millpond tick` runs every part whose schedule
is due, so one timer on a host serves every part there.

## Lines into git, raw files on the host

<Diagram name="lines" caption="One run of eddy's meeting part. The line becomes a commit; the transcript stays in the raw bank, behind a pointer the capture carries." />

A line holds what millpond checks and what enters git: the capture and,
for a capture that is its own excerpt, its signal. A raw file holds the
event as the source served it, whole: a transcript, the full release
notes, a log. It can run to megabytes. millpond banks it on the host,
outside git, and the capture points at it as `<host>:<path>`.

Three readers follow that pointer:

- a reader session, for which a meeting's transcript is the whole input;
- millpond's check that every excerpt a reader writes appears verbatim
  in the raw material;
- a person reading past the cut.

## The seven steps

<Diagram name="steps" caption="Four lines from one run. Each takes the steps in order, and a line that stops never stops the others." />

For every line, millpond:

1. **checks it**, and refuses a line with a bad key, a missing field or
   an excerpt with no text, naming it in the run;
2. **looks up the key** in every capture the inbox holds, and passes
   over a key it already holds;
3. **banks the raw files** on the host, under the capture's name;
4. **writes the capture**, with its pointer into the raw bank;
5. **writes the one signal** of a capture that carries an excerpt. It
   cuts the text at a line under the inbox's limit, 8 KiB unless the
   settings say otherwise, and closes it with what the raw record holds
   past the cut;
6. **marks the capture unread** when it carries no excerpt, which
   charges a reader;
7. **records the run**: its counts, its failures, its cursor and the
   status a fleet's doctor reads from `millpond status --json`.

A run that exits without `done` keeps every capture it already handed
over, since a capture lands once whatever happens. Its cursor stays
where it was, and millpond records the run as failed.

## Reading

A release is its own excerpt: its notes are the excerpt, and its signal
asks for a bump. A meeting is not, so a **reader**, a Claude session,
turns its transcript into signals through millpond's `read` tool.
millpond refuses a signal whose excerpt does not appear verbatim in the
capture's raw material. The reader's instructions are the inbox's own
data: what makes a signal, and how to name a speaker the transcript
leaves unnamed.

## Working a backlog

<Diagram name="backlog" caption="Nobody picks through hundreds of signals by hand. A session proposes, you change what you disagree with, and one commit writes the batch." />

The curate skill starts a Claude session that asks millpond's `signals`
tool for the oldest fifty unmoved signals, reads the raw material of the
unclear ones, and proposes one move for each. You accept the batch or
change rows, and the session writes it with one `moves` call, one
commit. The next batch starts where that one ended.

| Move | What follows |
|---|---|
| `drop` | millpond records the reason. |
| `answered` | The move names the claim or record that settled it. |
| `challenge` | The signal weighs against a claim. Only an inbox beside Flywheel's claims has one. |
| `join` | The signal makes an item, or grows a proposed one, with any number of others. |
| `attach` | The signal joins an item already sent. |
| `route` | The signal becomes a chore, an item of one, or an ask millpond records. |

An unmoved signal stays in the inbox until a move settles it, and its
weight counts by the date of its event.

## Where an item goes

An item is the unit of work: the signals curation joined, as five of a
meeting's ten, or two bumps that move together. millpond keeps its brief
and every place it was sent. `send` takes it to one of two places.

<Diagram name="send" caption="A question goes to a sounding, and its accepted conclusion becomes the item's record. The work the conclusion proposes becomes a new item, which goes to one worktree, and sluice's landing comes back to it." />

**One worktree.** `send` makes a worktree of the repository the send
names, on a branch named for the item, and writes the brief there. Then
it starts an agent on the brief, in a herdr pane or as a command it
prints. The agent submits its work through
[sluice](https://agentplot.github.io/sluice/), and millpond writes the
landing against the item from sluice's record of that branch.

**A sounding.** An item that asks a question ends in an answer, not a
change: whether to take a major release, or how to settle what a
meeting left open. `send` opens a sounding with the item's signals and
the captures they came from as its sources. One session works the
question in a worktree of its own, and ends on a conclusion: the answer,
its evidence, the work it proposes and what stays open. Code the
session writes there to test an answer never lands from it.

You decide the conclusion. Accept, and millpond keeps it as the item's
record and makes an item of each piece of work it proposes; curation
answers a later signal on the same subject by naming it. Go on, and the
sounding starts another round. Drop, and it records why. A finding the
sounding makes beyond its question comes back to millpond as a capture
of its own, keyed `sounding/<name>/<n>`, and takes the same path as
every other capture.

An inbox that crew keeps sends no item itself. Its settings hand items
to crew's planner, which orders them across teams and turns each into a
chore or a unit. Planning across items is crew's, and Flywheel's later,
and never millpond's.
